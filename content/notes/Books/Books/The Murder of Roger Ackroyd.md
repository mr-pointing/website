This one was a banger. Not totally perfect, but a banger. I loved Agatha Christie growing up, and thought her work was awesome. I never ended up reading a Hercule Poirot novel of my own when younger so a few years ago I added a bunch of his stories to my reading list. After "The Murder on the Orient Express," which I thought was kind of middle of the road, I took a short break. I'm happy to say I would love to read another one in the series. 

Hercule's character really grew on me. He's such a weirdo I can't help but love him. This book is also known for it's large twist, which I suspected from the beginning but didn't have a solid piece of evidence to back up my claim- just an inkling of a feeling. I won't spoil it here cause this genuinely such a quick and worthwhile read you have to experience it for yourself. Me talking about it won't do it enough justice.

The mindset I get in when reading these is that of over analysis. Everytime we learn a new fact, you have to make sure it fits in with the rest of the story. I was constantly flipping pages back and forth to corroborate someone's alibi. Even after doing this I still wasn't perceptive enough to figure it out myself.

Agatha is funny. Like really funny. Her sense of humor mixed with her eccentric characters keep the ball rolling. Every chapter has you wanting to start the next so you can figure out what's going on. The more and more I go on the more I want to delve into spoilers, so I'll keep this short and sweet. There's a pretty good reason this made it to many "Read this before you die!" lists, and I might add it to my own list of favorite novels. It introduced one of my favorite tropes of all time.
