Oh Brandon, how far you've come. I took a small break between these to read [Invisible Man]({{< ref "Invisible Man" >}}), which I'm grateful for. It made me reflect on the parts of [Mistborn]({{< ref "Mistborn" >}}) I liked and disliked. I'm happy to say that this entry improved on the flaws of the first one- for the most part. 

Vin and Elend make a decent couple. At first, they're completely insufferable, with one complaining until it's the others turn to do the same. Thankfully this is not the case for the entire book, but certainly the first half. To break this up, Sanderson finally gives us more character perspectives. These are so necessary, and start small but end up giving us great chapters. These aren't common enough to push this into Stormlight territory, but it's a much better attempt than the last.

The logical continuation of a world post Lord Ruler death makes perfect sense; Kelsier is dead and the world must go on. I do think at times maybe Sanderson bit off a bit more than he can chew, especially with one of the major antagonists, Zane. First of all, a love triangle with a brother was not the move. It came off as incredibly forced, and was very out of character for Vin to enjoy giving him so much attention. There was even a scene where Vin *vocalizes* her struggle to pick between the two. Honestly, could have left that out totally and it would have shown massive restraint, improving the story over all. But not everything can be perfect.

The issue of the map and world being underutilized is semi-relevant still, but like a lot of other elements here it is improved upon. Keeping things in Luthadel makes sense, since the main driving force of book 1, the Atium cache, is being sought after by a few different forces. Sazed continues to be an amazing character, and goes through one of the most believable and understandable character developments I've seen from Sanderson. I do wish we got a little more Feruchemy, but I won't complain too much. 

The ending left a little to be desired. I already started reading book 3 since I was so invested in trying to figure out more of what happened (which thankfully they explain pretty quick). Sometimes it feels as if I had no way of figuring out the motives behind decisions, but when the twist of how the main antagonistic force has been manipulating things the whole time was foretold in one of the first few chapters, I thought that was smart and well deserved. I have no idea how you could have predicted the use of unknown metals without them just explaining it, but again, you can't ask for everything. 

While the end of this book was amazing and is a 4 or even 5 out of 5, the whole first half is severely lacking and unjustly kind of boring. Therefore, this one will also be receiving a 3 out of 5 from me.
