The first in the Wheel of Time series, the series famously finished by Brandon Sanderson, opens up a world that got me hooked from the first 10 chapters. Being the first of nearly 14 books, I figured this would be dense and full of set up. It definitely was, but it was a lot easier to follow than I initially thought. It doesn't shy away from introducing elements that may or may not be expanded upon later, but from my perspective it wasn't over the top. It was the right amount of world-building and foreshadowing. 

That isn't to say it doesn't have its issues. Some of the characters fall a bit flat; Matt's character development of being so carefree he's susceptible to the evil of Shadar Logath is just too convenient. Rand is so stubborn to not accept his destiny he ends up forming his destiny. Perrin unfortunately has an extremely similar character arc; having two totally different characters experiencing two different but similar fates and having them both be so against it got very tiring towards the end. I also really don't understand Egwene's insistance on playing hard to get? To the point where you're unsure if she really does like Rand? Kind of rambling here, but you get my point. Not every character is written with the utmost care. 

Not a character complaint, but more so a logic one; clearly Aes Sedai are not trusted in the common world. However, after being saved by Morraine *countless* times, being brought to safety, and led to save the world, the party *still does not trust Morraine!* This really got me. Only Egwene seems to feel any sort of appreciation for what's been done- Nynaeve straight up wants to kill her. I don't know if these are fairly common troupes for 90's fantasy novels or what inspired a lot of later modern fiction, but they overstay their welcome. I've started book 2 and a lot of this one complaint is still relevant. 

The creature design and magic system is quite interesting. I think the idea of Trollocs sound fierce and having them be from different animals keeps the imagery interesting. Very 90's to have magic systems based entirely on biological sex, but what can you do. I think the gendered systems of the world are also an interesting take, where more women end up being in charge of the more important and logical decisions, which is pretty progressive. 

The writing between scenes is what kept me coming back. I really want to learn more about the world and how the One Power actually works. I also thought the Warder system and Wolf Pack were super cool. What kept this from being a stand out 4/5 was the speed at which the fight scenes were staged. Some pretty major fights with a good chunk of people involved are maybe 1 or 2 paragraphs. Sometimes a fight is brushed over so quickly I had to reread to make sure I didn't miss anything. Other than that, I'm really excited to continue reading and will probably **not** check out the Amazon show.
