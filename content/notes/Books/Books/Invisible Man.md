How does it feel to be free of illusion?

This was a powerhouse of a story. From the ominous beginning to the insanity that follows, Invisible Man proved itself as not just an engaging story, but a philosophical mirror, that asks questions you can't help but begin to ask yourself. Obviously this is related to the struggles and life of a black man in America, there are so many moments that felt relatable without having to have a shared experience. There were moments where you feel the anger of the character, feel the joy and wit in his comebacks to his ultimately outrageous situations. 

There were so many instances that felt so human. Whenever our MC moves throughout Harlem, getting into conversations, arguments and fights, that feel more normal than you'd expect out of a classic literature novel. I was expecting it to play it safe and be more reserved; quite the opposite happens. The beginning paints our character as a reserved, meek individual who cares about their image. As he matures, more so than he already was, he becomes more brash, more active, more reflective of the role he's been tasked with taking on. 

The commentary on a man's place in society, especially in one that rejects him openly, is brutally honest in it's presentation. Nearly every other character has direct control, or is in the midst of gaining direct control over our narrator. Speaking clearly, it made me upset. It made me want to step in and say something. I guess this is the point; making it clear how much our narrator also desires a different future for him. Towards the end we get what we want; his active participation in pushing against these controlling forces. Unfortunately that means reality steps in. Reality punishes his activity, sending him on the run and ending the story with a reflection on how it feels to be "free." 

The more and more I think about it, the more and more I want to reflect and discuss with others. I only know one other person who has read it that isn't the most social, so I haven't been able to reach out to anyone to talk about this with. I think that might be the part I'm missing most here. I have so much I want to talk about, without knowing where to start. This is definitely deserving of a reread eventually, as of all the classics I've read recently, this has been the most rewarding and enjoyable. Easy 5 out of 5.
