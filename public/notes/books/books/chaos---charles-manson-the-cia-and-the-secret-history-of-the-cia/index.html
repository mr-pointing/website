CHAOS - Charles Manson, the CIA, and the Secret History of the CIA

My first non-fiction read in quite a while, and one that made me want to pick up more of them. Not only has this book been on my “To be read” list for years, I had a coworker who read it and really sold me on it. I was skeptical at first; what would this book contain that I didn’t already suspect myself or imagine could be different than every other telling of the nights that Manson family slaughtered Sharon Tate and the like. I certainly did not expect what O’Neill presents: a narrative of negligence that potentially costed the lives of seemingly innocent people. Not only their negligence of letting this family almost openly commit crimes, but of the prosecution and handling of said family.

Without getting to deep into the content of the book, it should be stated that the read is engaging. The language is clear and concise without being bare bones simple. There were quite a few vocab words used by O’Neill I had to add to my personal dictionary. The flow between events makes sense, and the content rarely requires extensive knowledge in any areas outside of some pop culture of the 60’s. You really begin to feel for O’Neill as he gets deeper and deeper into a rabbit hole of his own creation.

My biggest takeaway from this (still ongoing) chain of events is that there is always going to be a level of accountability we will rarely see from the upper echelon. It’s tough to go into it without just recounting each discovery O’Neill uncovers, but take my word for it that everything that happened seems to have been completely preventable. Not in the, “Oh, I could have made it to work 10 minutes early had I woken up earlier” preventable, but the “Oh, a handful of people are dead. If only I had arrested this guy who said he’s going to kill them” preventable.
