Kill Creek

This was a somewhat decent attempt at writing a horror novel. Unfortunately, it fell flat in many, many, many directions. I don’t mind getting into spoilers because I cannot recommend this book to anyone. It’s slow, repetitive, and reliant and lot’s of tropes of the genre that hinder it more than help.

Our cast of characters are four somewhat-modern horror writers. They’re invited by the obvious red herring character who seems far too evil and knowledgeable to actually be evil or knowledgeable to stay a night in an obviously haunted house. Here’s the big kicker; this “evil” entity was created not by revenge or malice, but by superstition. This entity was willed into being by the story of the house. Which does sound cool, cause it kind of is! But there isn’t anything interesting done with this. The evil wants to do evil things because it was created by evil thoughts. No motivation other than just continuing to try and exist. It’s a great idea, but the actual cast of characters we interact with the entity through are so boring and surface-level nothing feels worth it.

We got the recluse mid-western boy who obviously hurt or killed his mom, the edgy woman going through abuse that loves to use phallic imagery in almost any context, the R.L. Stine for religion copycat, and of course, the Stephen King. They all act the way you think; main character has marital issues, the cast goes through trials and tribulations, and that’s about it. The writing was bland and played it extremely safe. The one idea I thought would be more interesting is the religious goosebumps clone ends up losing his daughter, which sets off his downward spiral into madness. Part of me almost wishes that he had been our main character, since he had so much more potential. Unfortunately, as soon as any set-up happens, it’s so obvious you could skip 20 pages and not miss much. This is the case for the entire book- this could have been a short story. It would have been much more worth my time, considering this is over 400 pages. I don’t think I’ll be checking out anything else by this author, anytime soon.
